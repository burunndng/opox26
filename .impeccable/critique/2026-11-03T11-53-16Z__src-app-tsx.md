## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for this product where it matters; category-interchangeable where it doesn't.**

The domain model and language are unmistakably opositor-specific: baremo (+1.00 / −0,25), "Regla de decisión del baremo," break-even rate (penalty/(1+penalty) = 20% for −0,25), "Confident-Wrong," "Reparar Ahora," error taxonomy E1–E20, Mode A/Mode B, FSRS retrieval pacing, cover-and-generate retrieval, legal-basis citations (TRLGSS), and an official "Corte Est. (50%) / APROBADO VIRTUAL" cut-off badge. The interaction model matches the real usage scene (distracted studier, repeated retrieval, keyboard-first micro-drills).

However, the visual execution is a slightly generic dark Tailwind admin shell: `bg-[#0c0f17]`/`#121722`/`#20293a` cards, slate-blue-indigo-rose accent palette, `font-legal-serif` headings that nod to "legal" character but are underused, rounded cards everywhere, small utility text. There's no strong visual identity beyond "clinical dark tool," and no sense of the real usage scene (early morning, tired, phone in hand). Stripped of the Spanish copy it could pass for an admin dashboard for any domain.

**Verdict:** Specific in domain model/scoring/error handling; interchangeable in chromatic shell, typography, and density. Missed opportunity: lean into the "laboratorio cognitivo" subtitle with deliberate visual character rather than default dark-dashboard.

## Overall Impression

A capable, domain-serious Operate-mode tool whose strongest moment is the session summary: instead of a cold grade, it reframes the −0,25 penalty into a calibration lesson ("arriesgar solo compensa si tu probabilidad de acierto supera el 20 %," "si hubieras dejado esas preguntas en blanco, tu nota neta habría sido +X pts"). The weakest moment is the first run: a first-timer lands on seven practice-mode buttons with no guidance, then encounters "Modo A / Modo B" and confidence calibration unexplained until after they've already made errors. The item screen is the highest-cognitive-load surface — the stem competes with ~10 simultaneous controls under −0,25 pressure — and that is the single biggest opportunity.

## What's Working

1. **Baremo-aware session summary with tactical audit** (confident-wrong, low-confidence gamble, break-even rule, "if abstained" counterfactual). Product-specific and pedagogically powerful: converts the official −0,25 penalty into a calibration feedback loop rather than a raw score. Corroborated by heuristic 9 = 4 in both assessments.
2. **Keyboard-first operation** (1–4, 0/b, space, reveal/next, `h` hint, `?` help, esc) combined with cover-and-generate retrieval before recognition — directly serves the distracted-studier / repeated-retrieval usage scene.
3. **Error taxonomy E1–E20 + ErrorRepairStudio + printable Dossier de Fallos y Trampas** — a concrete artifact for error analysis, a real oposiciones study method rather than a generic feature.
4. **Paused-session resume banner on Dashboard** — rare, well-executed UX (Reanudar sesión).

## Priority Issues

**[P0] ActivityRunner item-screen control-bar overload.**
- **What:** During the primary answering task, the stem competes with ~10 simultaneous controls (mode badge, baremo row, live net score, streak, progress bar, generative toggle, pacing timer, export, save, exit, FSRS/badge/difficulty pills). The user can't tell what's decision-relevant vs. decorative.
- **Why it matters:** Violates single focus, chunking, grouping, one-thing-at-a-time, and progressive disclosure at the moment of highest cognitive load (answering under −0,25 penalty pressure). A distracted studier will misclick or freeze. Assessment B corroborates via ActivityRunner's 3159-line monolith + ~11 `useEffect` hooks + multiple global window listeners.
- **Fix:** Collapse secondary controls into a collapsible panel/"more" menu; during answering show only exit + baremo/net-score context (relevant to the penalty decision). Reveal pacing/export/save on demand.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

**[P1] Modal accessibility: no `aria-modal`, no focus trapping/restoration.**
- **What:** None of the modal components (`HelpShortcutsModal`, `InterleavedBlockModal`, `CreateTopicModal`, `JevLabModal`, `ExportSettingsMethodModal`) set `aria-modal="true"` or implement focus containment/restoration. `aria-modal` returns zero matches across all `*Modal*.tsx`. `HelpShortcutsModal`'s close `×` button lacks `aria-label`. (Assessment B, STEP 3.)
- **Why it matters:** A keyboard/screen-reader user can tab out of an open modal into the background; screen readers may not treat overlays as dialogs. WCAG breach.
- **Fix:** Add `role="dialog"` + `aria-modal="true"` to all modals, trap focus on open, restore focus on close, label close buttons.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Decision-point option overload (>4 visible options).**
- **What:** StudySetup presents 7 practice-mode buttons simultaneously. ActivityRunner's baremo row shows multiple BAREMOS preset buttons. The top bar shows ~6 buttons. All exceed the ≤4 working-memory rule.
- **Why it matters:** Forces scanning/comparison before acting; increases error risk and decision fatigue on the most frequent path (start a session).
- **Fix:** Reduce StudySetup to the smart default + 2–3 clearly secondary options; route the rest into "Otras formas de practicar." Limit baremo presets shown at once.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

**[P2] No onboarding / empty-state guidance for first-time users.**
- **What:** A first-timer lands on StudySetup with 7 practice options and zero guidance on which to pick. "Modo A / Modo B" and FSRS terms surface unexplained; confidence 0–3 matters but isn't introduced before the first item. Help covers shortcuts only. No empty state when nothing is due.
- **Why it matters:** First-timers learn the mental model (baremo, confidence calibration, Mode A encoding) only after they've already made errors — too late for trust-building in a precision tool.
- **Fix:** Add a short first-run onboarding (~60 s) demonstrating confidence calibration on a practice item, plus empty states and tooltips for Mode A vs B.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

**[P3] Visual density and small type.**
- **What:** `text-[10px]`/`text-[11px]` labels throughout; ~10 concurrent badges on the item screen; inconsistent border-radius; Assessment B found small `text-slate-500` captions on dark card surfaces (`#121722`/`#182030`) fall below 4.5:1 contrast (≈3.2–4.3:1), which the detector missed.
- **Why it matters:** Reduces scanability for a tired, possibly mobile studier; inconsistent radius reads as unpolished and erodes trust in a precision tool.
- **Fix:** Tighten the badge system, raise minimum body text, unify radius, fix small-caption contrast, ensure ≥44 px touch targets.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable quieter`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power user) — primary action: run a fast SRS due session.**
- Baremo locks after the first answer with no mid-session escape; can't experiment with penalty settings without abandoning the session.
- No command palette / fast switching; session save/pause requires modal clicks rather than a single hotkey despite being a repeat user.
- Keyboard shortcuts (`h`, confidence 0–3) aren't labeled on the UI — must open the `?` modal to remember them.

**Jordan (first-timer) — primary action: start first practice session.**
- Lands on StudySetup with 7 practice-mode buttons and zero guidance on which to choose; the smart default is present but not explained as *the* choice.
- "Modo A / Modo B" and FSRS terms surface with no prior explanation; learned only after encountering them.
- Doesn't know confidence 0–3 affects scoring until after the first item; no warning that a confident-wrong triggers "quarantine."
- No empty-state guidance when nothing is due — unclear what to do next.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent) — primary action: review errors via keyboard/screen reader.**
- `text-[10px]`/`text-[11px]` labels below comfortable reading size; `text-slate-500` captions on dark card surfaces ≈ 3.2–4.3:1, below 4.5:1 for small text (Assessment B; detector missed this).
- Modals lack `aria-modal` and focus trapping — tab can escape an open dialog.
- `focus:outline-none` without guaranteed visible replacement (`focus:ring-0` in JevLabModal/ExportSettingsMethodModal).
- Color-coded status (emerald/rose/amber) carries meaning without sufficient redundant text in several badges/pills.

**Riley (stress tester) — primary action: take a penalized simulacro.**
- Net score can go negative and displays in rose with "NO SUPERA EL CORTE" — high-anxiety moment with no "pause without penalty" escape mid-item other than blank.
- Pacing timer with `animate-pulse` at ≤10 s adds explicit time pressure (E10) that could panic rather than train.
- No undo on a confidence misclick — clicking 3 instead of 2 creates a permanent confident-wrong record.
- Live net-score pill updates after every answer, turning the session into a constantly shifting scoreboard under penalty.

**Casey (distracted mobile) — primary action: quick micro-drill on phone.**
- Item-screen top control bar is a long horizontal flex that wraps awkwardly on small screens.
- Cover-stage textarea cramped on mobile; many buttons `px-2.5 py-1.5 text-xs` — likely under the 44 px touch-target minimum.
- `hidden md:inline` text on toggles leaves icons-only affordances unclear at small sizes.
- No persistent indication of which shortcut works on mobile (no external keyboard assumed).

## Minor Observations

- Loading skeleton on boot is well done (`aria-busy`, `aria-live="polite"`, `sr-only` status text).
- Toast auto-dismisses after 5 s — reasonable and non-blocking.
- Baremo "fijado al iniciar la sesión" transparency communicates integrity.
- The E1–E20 legend inside a native `<details>` element is accessible but visually plain; could be a first-class reference.
- "Pulsa Espacio para continuar" appears in both cover reveal and feedback — consistent shortcut language.
- `font-legal-serif` headings nod to the legal domain but body remains sans, so the "legal" character never fully lands.
- Detector's 12 findings were all false positives or subjective slop calls (near-black `slate-950` flagged as "gray"; indigo accents on dark labeled "AI palette" despite passing contrast). Zero actionable detector findings.

## Provocative Questions to Consider

1. Should the baremo really lock after the first answer — or should the app show a "what-if" re-score at session end so learners can experiment with penalty sensitivity without corrupting attempt data?
2. Could the entire top control bar move into a collapsible panel so the item stem gets 100 % of the visual field during answering, with baremo/net score revealed only as context for the penalty decision?
3. Should a first-time user pass through a ~60-second onboarding demonstrating confidence calibration on a practice item before their first real session — to prevent the "confident-wrong surprise" valley?
4. Is "Confident-Wrong" too punitive as a first-encounter label? Would reframing it as "Sobreconfianza detectable" with a softer tone reduce avoidance of the Repair Studio?
